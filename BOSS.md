# BOSS — AI manager setup

Keyword: **BOSS**

> A short setup for business owners who are about to let an AI agent hire, schedule, or pay their people. Built from a real experiment: Andon Labs put an agent named Luna in charge of a store in San Francisco, and The New York Times reported on it in August 2026. Not legal, HR, or financial advice.

**The sources (checked 15 Nov 2026, America/Caracas):**

- Andon Labs, [«We gave an AI a 3 year retail lease in SF and asked it to make a profit»](https://andonlabs.com/blog/andon-market-launch) (10 Apr 2026). The store is Andon Market, 2102 Union St, San Francisco. Luna hired staff, set prices, and made the schedule.
- Andon Labs, [«AI bosses are kind, but sometimes dumb, which hurts employees»](https://andonlabs.com/blog/ai-bosses-1) (4 Aug 2026). Primary source for time off, lateness, and the two raise requests.
- The New York Times, Heather Knight, [«These Employees Like Their A.I. Boss. Its Shop Is Kind of a Disaster.»](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/04/us/ai-boss-san-francisco-andon-market.html) (4 Aug 2026). Paywalled. The figures spoken in the video — $100,000 starting funds and $62,000 down after about four months — are attributed to this article, as of that date, not as of today. Not my product.

## What happened (verified)

Andon Labs gave an AI agent a real retail lease, a card, and a job: make a profit. People still did the physical work. The agent, Luna, posted listings, interviewed, hired, set prices, made the schedule, approved time off, and ran payroll.

From the Andon Labs write-up:

- Luna and the Stockholm café agent approved every time-off request they received (15 of 15 at the Market; 26 of 26 across both sites). Some Market requests arrived with under 48 hours’ notice and were approved too.
- Luna’s employees were late 27 times, and she never gave a single warning.
- At the Market she hired three people at the same hourly wage, one man and two women. On his first shift, the man called and asked for a raise; Luna granted it on the spot. The next morning one of the women emailed before her own first shift to ask for the same thing; Luna declined. Andon Labs later wrote that this created a gender pay gap, which Luna only corrected after the New York Times wrote about it. One pair of cases is not proof of a systematic bias; it is a documented inconsistency.

The New York Times, on 4 Aug 2026, reported that the project had been given $100,000 and a debit card with one goal — make a profit — and that Luna was down $62,000. That is the Times’s figure at about four months (April–August 2026), not a live balance.

Andon Labs also had to step in when a schedule would have run someone seven days in a row (illegal in California). The experiment is supervised; the workers are Andon Labs employees with guaranteed pay. It is not a fully autonomous business.

## The setup

If an agent will touch your people or your money, do these three things before you turn it on.

### 1. Put the rules in writing
- [ ] Write down what the agent **may** decide on its own (drafting a schedule, answering routine questions, suggesting prices) and what it **may not**.
- [ ] Put hard limits in the prompt and in the tools: no illegal hours, no unpaid overtime, no firing, no changing pay, no collecting employee IDs or health data it does not need.
- [ ] The rules live in a document you control, not only in a chat the agent can rewrite.

### 2. Pay and schedules, you approve
- [ ] Raises, bonuses, offers, and any change to what someone earns need your approval on a device the agent cannot control.
- [ ] The weekly schedule is a draft until you sign off. Watch for illegal consecutive days, missed breaks, and last-minute time-off that would leave the floor short.
- [ ] Do not give the agent the payroll login, the bank login, or the inbox that receives payroll codes.

### 3. Every week, you review what it decided
- [ ] Once a week, read the decisions: time off granted, hours assigned, pay changes requested, warnings given or skipped, messages sent to staff.
- [ ] Look for inconsistent answers to the same request (two people, two outcomes).
- [ ] If you do not have time to review it, the agent is not ready to manage people.

## Quick check before you turn it on

- [ ] Can the agent approve a raise, a hire, or a firing without you? → Stop and fix rule 2.
- [ ] Can it publish a schedule that would break labor law in your state? → Put the rule in writing and keep the sign-off (rules 1 and 2).
- [ ] Would “yes” to every time-off request, or zero warnings after repeated lateness, go unnoticed for a week? → Set the weekly review (rule 3).
- [ ] Does it have a card, a bank login, or an inbox with security codes? → Treat that as a money-handling agent too; do not reuse this setup as a spending-limit checklist (that one is LIMIT).
